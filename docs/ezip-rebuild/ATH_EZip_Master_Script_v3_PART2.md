## 🟡 SLIDE 6 — ECLIPSE® AUTHORIZED DEALER
### `e06` · `ez-eclipse` · splittext · **NEW SLIDE**

👉 Establish the manufacturer as a real company that will still exist in fifteen years
👉 Answers the unspoken fear behind every cheap bid — an orphaned warranty
⏱ 1:15 – 1:30

**Tone/Mood:** Steady and factual. This is a heritage slide, so let the dates and the places do
the work. The rep should sound like he finds this genuinely interesting, not like he's reciting a
brochure.

### Block 1 — Who builds it

> "So that's us. Let me tell you who actually builds the product.
>
> Eclipse Shading Systems is out of Middletown, New York, with additional manufacturing down in
> Statesville, North Carolina. They've been building the E-Zip side-retention system since 2013,
> and the larger seven-inch line since 2019.
>
> Everything is custom made in the USA, built to the inch for your specific opening. There's
> nothing off the shelf in this."

### Block 2 — Why the manufacturer matters

> "Here's why I bring that up.
>
> When you buy something like this, you're really trusting two companies. The one who installs it
> and the one who builds it. If either one disappears, your warranty goes with them.
>
> A lot of the cheaper systems out there are assembled from parts sourced from three or four
> different suppliers. Nobody owns the whole thing. When something fails in year six, you're
> chasing a component manufacturer overseas.
>
> With this, it's one product family from one manufacturer, and we're the licensed and insured
> authorized dealer for it right here. Two companies, both accountable, both still going to be
> here."

**Transition out:** "Let me show you what that actually gets you."

**Display beats:**
- Middletown NY, manufacturing in Statesville NC
- E-Zip since 2013 · 7-inch since 2019
- Custom made in the USA, built to the inch
- You're trusting two companies — installer and manufacturer
- Cheap systems = parts from four suppliers, nobody owns it
- One product family, one manufacturer, licensed local dealer

**Slow down on:** *"If either one disappears, your warranty goes with them."*
Why: it's the real fear behind a low bid and it's the one thing a cheap competitor genuinely
cannot answer. Say it plainly, don't oversell it, move on.

**Coaching note:** Homeowners worry about two failures — the product breaking, and the company
vanishing. Slides 2 through 5 answered the second one about ATH. This slide answers it about
Eclipse, and it matters more than reps think.

Say the specific places out loud. "Middletown, New York" and "Statesville, North Carolina" sound
like real towns where real people build things. "American-made" by itself sounds like marketing.

Do not turn this into a product slide. The product gets sold in Tab 3. This slide's only job is
making the manufacturer feel permanent.

## 🟡 SLIDE 7 — WHY ECLIPSE
### `e07` · `ez-credibility` · credibility *(was `e05`)*

👉 Stack proof fast — engineering, motor, fabric, wind
👉 Every badge needs a "which means" or it's decoration
⏱ 2:00 – 2:15

**Tone/Mood:** Brisk and confident. This is a stack, so keep it moving — one item, one sentence of
meaning, next. The one place to slow down is wind, because that's the item tied to something the
homeowner has personally watched happen in their own backyard.

### Block 1 — The product line

> "A few things worth knowing about what you're actually getting.
>
> Eclipse has been building this exact system since 2013 — residential and commercial, all over
> the country. It's a refined product at this point, not a first attempt. One consistent product
> family, custom made to the inch."

### Block 2 — The motor

> "The motor is Somfy, and it comes standard. Same brand used in premium motorized systems
> worldwide. That's not a small thing — the motor is the part that has to work ten thousand times
> without you thinking about it.
>
> Handheld remote, wireless wall switch, or your phone. And it'll integrate with home automation
> if you ever want to go that direction."

### Block 3 — The fabric

> "The fabric is Phifer SunTex. Phifer is one of the most respected shading fabric manufacturers
> in the world, and SunTex is GREENGUARD Gold certified — which means it's been independently
> tested for what it off-gasses, and it passed the strictest tier.
>
> On top of the Eclipse warranty, Phifer backs the fabric with their own ten-year exterior
> warranty. So the fabric has two companies behind it too."

### Block 4 — Wind

> "And this is the one that matters most out here.
>
> The four and five inch systems carry a Class 6 wind rating, tested to eighty miles per hour, and
> they carry Miami-Dade County approval — that's the hurricane standard."

*(Localize. Use their house.)*

> "You mentioned the wind rips through here in the afternoon — that's exactly what that rating is
> about.
>
> What makes it work is the zipper retention. The fabric edge is physically locked into the side
> track, so instead of billowing and flapping like a generic roll-down screen, it stays flat and
> taut."

**Transition out:** "Let me show you some of these in the wild."

**Display beats:**
- E-Zip since 2013 — refined, not a first attempt
- Somfy motor STANDARD — the part that has to work 10,000 times
- Phifer SunTex, GREENGUARD Gold — plus Phifer's own 10-year fabric warranty
- Class 6, 80 MPH, Miami-Dade approval
- LOCALIZE the wind line to their house, every time
- Zipper retention = flat and taut, not flapping

**Slow down on:** the wind localization.
Why: this is the moment an engineering number becomes a thing the homeowner has watched happen on
their own patio. A rep who reads "Class 6, 80 MPH" and moves on has wasted the strongest item on
the slide.

**Coaching note:** Every badge needs a "which means" attached to it. Miami-Dade means nothing to a
Colorado homeowner until you say hurricane standard. GREENGUARD Gold means nothing until you say
independently tested and passed the strictest tier. A new rep reads the badge and moves on — the
translation is the entire job of this slide.

Keep it moving otherwise. Four items in two minutes means roughly thirty seconds each. If you're
still on the motor at the ninety second mark, you're going to lose the room before you get to
wind, which is the one they actually care about.

**Reactive script — only if they ask about reviews or ratings:**

> "Eclipse publishes their own customer review data — last I saw it was around 99% of owners
> saying they'd recommend it, with product and dealer ratings both in the high fours. I'd say
> that's per Eclipse's published reviews rather than something I can independently verify, but
> it's consistent with what we see."

👉 Attribute it. Do not state these as your own numbers, and do not put them on a slide.

**Verification:**
- ⚠️ Eclipse feefo statistics — 99.2% would recommend, 4.9 product, 4.8 dealer. Eclipse-published,
  not independently verified. Rep-only, attributed, never on a customer slide. Blocked on `oi04`.

**Flags:**
- 🟡 *medium* — The deck row currently reads "Tested to 80 mph — 4" & 5" systems," which correctly
  excludes the 7-inch but leaves an obvious gap a sharp homeowner will notice. Resolve alongside
  `oi01`.

## 🟡 SLIDE 8 — REAL PROJECTS
### `e08` · `ez-gallery` · photogrid *(was `e11`)*
### *Right Here on the Front Range*

👉 Localization equals credibility — closes "have you done work around here?" before it's asked
👉 Proof that their exact problem has already been solved for someone nearby
⏱ 1:00 – 1:15

**Tone/Mood:** Relaxed and specific. This is show-and-tell, not a pitch. Slow the pace — photos
are one of the few moments a homeowner is genuinely looking at the screen instead of at the rep.

### Block 1

> "These are all local. Every one of these is a project we've done on the Front Range."

*(Point to the photo closest to their situation.)*

> "This one here is very similar to what you've got — same exposure, same kind of opening, same
> problem with the afternoon sun.
>
> Here's what it looks like with the screens up. You barely notice it's there. Clean cassette, low
> profile, looks like it was designed with the house.
>
> And here's the same space with them down."

*(On multi-opening projects.)*

> "This one's a full enclosure — multiple screens across multiple openings. That homeowner
> basically turned their patio into an outdoor room. They use it most of the year now."

**Transition out:** "And it's not just a handful of projects — let me show you where these are."

**Display beats:**
- Every photo is local — say it
- Point to the ONE closest to their situation and say why
- Up: barely visible, looks designed-in
- Down: the space is transformed
- Multi-opening: outdoor room, used most of the year

**Slow down on:** the tie-back to their situation.
Why: don't say it while advancing the slide. Say it, stop, and let them look at the photo while
the comparison sinks in.

**Coaching note:** The whole value of this slide is that it's their neighborhood and their problem
on somebody else's house. A generic "here are some installs" wastes it.

Before the appointment, pick the two photos closest to this customer — same exposure, same deck
style, same part of town. If you can name the general area and they recognize it, that's worth
more than any badge in the deck.

### `e09` · `ez-refmap` · refmap · **NEW SLIDE**

👉 Turn "have you done work around here?" into a visual answer
👉 Sets up an asset you'll return to during price conditioning
⏱ 45 seconds – 1:00

**Tone/Mood:** Easy and conversational. The map does the talking. Let the homeowner look for their
own street — that's the moment that lands, and it only happens if the rep stops talking.

### Block 1

> "We've completed projects all over the greater Colorado Springs area — Monument, Falcon, Black
> Forest, the Springs, out toward Peyton.
>
> Every home is a little different, but at this point we've worked through just about every
> scenario there is."

*(Show the map. Then stop talking for five or six seconds.)*

**Transition out:** "Alright — that's the company and the product. Let's talk about your space."

**Display beats:**
- Projects across the whole greater Springs area — name the towns
- Show the map
- STOP TALKING for five or six seconds
- If they find their neighborhood, ask about it

**Slow down on:** the silence after you show the map.
Why: homeowners almost always start hunting for their own street on their own. When they say "oh,

**Coaching note:** This is one of the few assets that gets used twice in an appointment. It's proof
here, and it comes back during price conditioning when you're explaining why a company doing this
volume can price the way it does. Don't burn it — show it, let them react, move on.

A rep who over-explains the map has nothing left to bring back to the table.

say that if it's true.

## 🟡 SLIDE 10 — WHAT'S PUSHING YOU BACK INSIDE?
### `e10` · `ez-reasons` · reasonsphoto *(was `e06`)*

👉 THE most important transition in the deck — pivot from company to their space
👉 The Accomplish List gets confirmed and owned here. Nothing proceeds until it does.
⏱ 2:00 – 2:30

**Tone/Mood:** Warmer and slower than the trust tab. The rep stops presenting and starts
listening. This is a conversation slide — if the homeowner isn't talking by the end of it, it's
being run wrong.

### Block 1 — Name the common problems

> "So most of the homeowners we do screen projects for are dealing with some mix of the same four
> things."

*(Walk the hotspots. Do not read all four in order like a list — touch the ones that match what they've told you.)*

> "**Sun and glare.** Blocking the harsh afternoon or morning sun without losing the feeling of
> being outside — and cutting the heat and glare that reaches the rooms behind you.
>
> **Wind.** The fabric edge zips into the side tracks, so it stays flat instead of flapping. The
> space becomes somewhere you can actually sit.
>
> **Privacy.** A darker fabric reads almost like a mirror from outside during the day. Neighbors
> can't see in. You can still see out.
>
> **Bugs.** The tight SunTex weave plus the brush seal on the hem bar keeps no-see-ums and
> mosquitoes out. For a lot of people that alone changes how much they use the space in the
> evening."

### Block 2 — The Accomplish List confirmation

> "For you, it sounds like the main things are… `{{ACCOMPLISH_LIST}}`"

*(List their items. Their words, not yours. Not product names.)*

> "Does that feel right? Did I miss anything?"

👉 **Wait for confirmation. Do not skip this and do not rush it.**

> "Perfect. Everything I'm about to show you is built around those specifically."

### Block 3 — Frame question *(ask regardless)*

> "One more thing — what sort of stuff do you all like to do out here?"

*(If the answer is short: dinner? drinks? grandkids? the dog?)*

> "That's really what this is about. Getting you more of those days."

**Transition out:** "So let me show you how the system actually works."

**Display beats:**
- Four common problems: sun/glare · wind · privacy · bugs
- Touch only the ones that match THEIR situation
- Say their Accomplish List back in THEIR words
- "Did I miss anything?" — then wait
- Frame question: what do you like to do out here?
- "Everything I show you is built around those goals"

**Slow down on:** the Accomplish List callback.
Why: say their items back in their own words, then stop. The silence is productive — it's the
moment the homeowner realizes this is about their house and not about a product.

**Coaching note:** This is where the presentation turns. Slides 1 through 9 were about us. From
here on, every single feature you show has to answer something said on this slide.

New reps treat the Accomplish List callback as a formality and rush it. Don't. If the list feels
thin, the frame question fixes it — ask what they like to do out here, listen, and add whatever
comes loose before you move on.

Never present a feature later that doesn't connect back to something said here. If it doesn't map
to their list, it's a spec.

**Engagement question:** *"What sort of things do you all like to do out here?"*
— Draw it out if needed: dinner, drinks, cards, the grandkids, the dog. This question produces
more usable Accomplish List material than any other in the appointment.

**Callback triggers:**
- Immediately before presenting price — recap this list first, always
- Any objection where value needs rebuilding
- Homeowner says "we need to think about it"

## 🟡 SLIDE 11 — HOW THE E-ZIP WORKS
### `e11` · `ez-how` · splittext *(was `e07`)*

👉 Give them a mental model simple enough to repeat to someone who isn't in the room
👉 Zipper retention is your single clearest competitive differentiator — this is where it lands
⏱ 1:15 – 1:30

**Tone/Mood:** Light and hands-on. Use the diagram, use the track sample, use your hands. This is
the most satisfying slide in the deck to deliver because the mechanism is genuinely clever and it
takes about ninety seconds to explain.

### Block 1 — Top to bottom

> "Here's the whole system, without getting into the weeds.
>
> **At the top**, the screen rolls up and stores inside a cassette — a protective aluminum housing.
> When it's not in use, rain, UV, and pine needles never touch your fabric. That's a big part of
> why these last.
>
> **You press a button** and the screen comes down. And here's the part that matters —"

*(Hand them the track sample.)*

> "— the fabric edge has a zipper welded onto it, and that zipper rides inside the side track.
> It's physically locked in. That's the whole difference between this and a generic roll-down
> screen.
>
> A screen with no zipper track is held at the top and the bottom and nowhere else. First real
> gust, it billows out, gaps open along the sides, and eventually it tears itself up. This one
> can't. The edge is captured for the entire drop.
>
> **At the bottom**, the hem bar has a brush seal that meets the floor. So when it's all the way
> down you've got a real seal — no gap along the deck."

### Block 2 — Control

> "And it doesn't have to be all the way down. Stop it wherever you want. Partial shade, partial
> wind block, full enclosure — you're in control of it.
>
> Up, it disappears. Down, the space is protected. That's really it."

**Transition out:** "So that's how they all work. Where they start to differ is in how they're
built — let's look at what fits your openings."

**Display beats:**
- Top: cassette housing — rain, UV, needles never touch fabric
- HAND THEM THE TRACK before you explain the zipper
- Zipper welded to fabric edge, rides inside the track, physically locked
- No-zipper screens: held top and bottom only → billows, gaps, tears
- Bottom: hem bar brush seal meets the floor, no gap
- Stops at any height — partial or full, you're in control
- "Up it disappears, down it's protected"

**Slow down on:** *"A screen with no zipper track is held at the top and the bottom and nowhere
else."*
Why: this is the sentence that makes every cheaper bid look cheap, and it does it without naming
anyone. It's a physical fact about how the competing product is built, which is far more powerful
than an opinion about its quality.

**Coaching note:** Use the zipper explanation every single time. It is your clearest competitive
differentiator and it's the thing the homeowner will repeat to a spouse who isn't in the room, or
to the next company that quotes them.

Get the track sample into their hands before you explain it, not after. People understand the
mechanism about four times faster when they're holding it, and the reveal holds their attention.

A homeowner who can explain zipper retention to somebody else is doing your follow-up for you.

## 🟡 SLIDE 13 — THE FABRIC
### `e13` · `ez-fabric` · splittext *(was `e09`)*
### *SunTex® by Phifer*

👉 Make it personal — this is the first slide where they're choosing something
👉 Ownership starts here. A homeowner who has picked a color has started owning the project.
⏱ 1:30 – 1:45

**Tone/Mood:** Collaborative and unhurried. Samples out, book open. The rep is helping them decide
something, not explaining something. This should feel like the fun part, because it is.

### Block 1 — One manufacturer, one decision

> "So every fabric we use is Phifer SunTex. GREENGUARD Gold certified, built specifically for
> exterior use.
>
> And here's the good news — all the colors cost exactly the same. So price isn't part of this
> decision at all. The only real choice is how much UV you want it to block versus how much you
> want to see through."

### Block 2 — The 95 *(hand them the sample)*

> "This is SunTex 95, and it's what most of our customers land on.
>
> The number is the UV blockage — this blocks about ninety-five percent of the UV, which means
> it's only about five percent open. Look through it."

*(Let them. Stop talking.)*

> "You keep your view. The heat and glare are gone, but you're not staring at a wall.
>
> And here's the part people love — from the outside during the day, it reads almost like a
> mirror. Your neighbors can't see in. You can see out. A lot of folks end up valuing that as much
> as the sun control."

### Block 3 — The alternatives

> "If you want more than that, the 97 blocks a little more and gives you a bit more privacy.
>
> And if you want true blackout — total privacy, or a really brutal west exposure — we can go to a
> solid fabric instead.
>
> Color-wise you've got six options on the 80 and 90, and ten on the 95 and 97. Near-black all the
> way to white."

*(Open the SunTex book.)*

> "Most homes out here look best picking up the trim or the fascia. What color is your trim?"

**Transition out:** "Let me show you what this actually looks like on a real house."

**Display beats:**
- All Phifer SunTex, GREENGUARD Gold — one manufacturer, no surprises
- ALL COLORS SAME PRICE — take budget off the table immediately
- Hand them the 95 and let them look through it — then stop talking
- The number is UV BLOCKAGE, not openness. 95 blocks ~95%, is ~5% open.
- Mirror effect from outside by day = privacy without losing the view
- 97 for more blockage · solid fabric for true blackout
- 6 colors on 80/90, 10 colors on 95/97
- Open the book. Ask about their trim color.

**Slow down on:** the moment you hand them the 95 and they look through it.
Why: do not narrate while they're doing it. This is the single most convincing thirty seconds in
the presentation and it works entirely without you.

**Coaching note:** ⚠️ **Never say "95% openness."** It's backwards and it's the kind of error a
competitor or a sharp homeowner will catch. SunTex 95 *blocks* about 95% of UV and is only about
5% open. The number in the name is blockage.

Bring the physical book to every appointment. A rep who describes fabric options verbally instead
of handing over samples has turned the most engaging slide in the deck into a lecture.

Saying "all the colors cost the same" early is deliberate. It removes price from a decision the
homeowner is about to make, which means they choose what they actually want — and people defend
choices they actually wanted.

**Reactive script:**

> **"Will it fade?"**
>
> "SunTex is solution-dyed, so the color is built into the fiber rather than printed on the
> surface. It doesn't fade off the way a coated fabric does. That's part of why Phifer backs it
> with a ten-year exterior warranty on top of the Eclipse coverage."

## 🟡 SLIDE 14 — BEFORE · AFTER · INSIDE
### `e14` · `ez-transform` · photogrid · **NEW SLIDE**

👉 The single most persuasive visual in the deck — one project, three views
👉 Answers "will I feel closed in?" before they ask it
⏱ 1:00 – 1:15

**Tone/Mood:** Let the photos work. Minimal talking. The rep's job here is to point and then be
quiet.

### Block 1 — Before

> "Same house, three views. This is before — nice space, no protection. Wide open to the sun and
> whatever the wind is doing."

### Block 2 — After

> "Same patio, screens installed and down. Look at how clean that reads. It doesn't look like
> something got added on — it looks like it belongs there."

### Block 3 — Inside *(the important one)*

> "And this is the one I really want you to see. This is standing inside, looking out through the
> screen.
>
> You can still see the neighborhood. You can see the sky. The light is soft instead of blinding,
> and the wind is off you — but you're clearly still outside.
>
> That's the ninety-five doing exactly what it's supposed to do."

**Transition out:** "So that's the system. Let me show you how you actually run it."

**Display beats:**
- Before: nice space, no protection, wide open
- After: clean, intentional, looks like it belongs
- Inside: THE money photo — still see out, soft light, still outside
- Say less here than feels natural

**Slow down on:** the Inside photo.
Why: every homeowner is quietly worried about feeling boxed in. This photo answers it completely
and permanently, and it does it better than any sentence you could construct.

**Coaching note:** If their space is genuinely similar to this project, say so directly — "this is
almost exactly what we'd be doing for you." If it isn't, don't stretch it. An overclaimed
comparison the homeowner can see through costs more than the photo gains.

This slide is also your pre-emptive answer to the cage objection. If you deliver it well, that
objection often never comes up. If it comes up anyway, come back here rather than arguing.

**Callback triggers:**
- "Is it going to feel like a cage?"
- "Will we still be able to see the view?"
- Any hesitation about the space feeling closed in
