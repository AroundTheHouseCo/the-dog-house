⏱ 1:15 – 1:30

**Tone/Mood:** Reassuring and organized. The rep should sound like he's describing a system that

pressure, not raise interest.

### Block 2 — Shade Service

> "And then there's one more thing most companies don't do.
>
> **Twelve months after your install, we come back out.** Free. We clean the system, lubricate the
> tracks, and inspect everything. It's not a sales visit, it's just how we keep these running the
> way they should.
>
> That's the part people tell us about later."

### Block 3 — Lead time

> "Timeline-wise, we're running about six to eight weeks from order to install on screen
> projects. Everything is custom built to your openings, so that's manufacturing time more than
> scheduling."

**Transition out:** "And backing all of that is the warranty, which is worth a minute on its own."

**Display beats:**
- Design → PM assigned → Install → Walkthrough
- HOA packet handled for you if needed
- Most installs one day, large enclosures maybe two
- 12-MONTH SHADE SERVICE — free, we come back, clean/lubricate/inspect
- 6 to 8 weeks, custom build time not scheduling backlog
- Deliver it like a promise, not a checklist

**Slow down on:** *"Twelve months after your install, we come back out. Free."*
Why: nobody expects it, and it reframes the whole purchase from a transaction into a relationship.
Say it, pause, let them react.

**Coaching note:** This slide feels like housekeeping to a new rep, so it gets rushed. It
shouldn't be. Home improvement is the most complained-about industry in the country and the
complaints are almost never about the product — they're about nobody calling back, nobody showing
up, and nobody knowing who's responsible. Every step here is quietly answering one of those.

Frame the six to eight weeks as build time, not backlog. "It's being manufactured for your
openings" is a completely different sentence than "we're booked out."

**Engagement question:** *"Have you had work done on the home before? How'd that go?"*
— Use it here if you didn't use it at slide 4. If it went badly, that's your opening: this
industry is notorious for exactly that, which is why the process runs the way it does.

### 🔒 REP-ONLY — The PPP downgrade lever

*Never presented on this slide. Used only during right-sizing, after price, if the homeowner needs
to reduce scope and has already declined to remove openings.*

Standard PPP: lifetime frame, fabric, motor, electronics.

Downgraded coverage becomes:
- **Frame** — lifetime *(unchanged)*
- **Fabric** — 10 years
- **Motor** — 8 years
- **Electronics and finish** — 5 years

**How to offer it:**

> "There is one other lever. The warranty package we price in is the lifetime coverage across the
> board. There's a step-down version — frame stays lifetime, fabric goes to ten years, motor to
> eight, electronics and finish to five.
>
> I'll be honest, most people don't take it, because the lifetime coverage is a big part of why
> they chose us. But it's there if the number needs to come down and you'd rather keep all the
> openings."

👉 **Order of operations at the close:** remove openings first, step down duty level second,
downgrade warranty third. Never lead with the warranty — it's the one that costs the customer the
most in the long run.

## 🟡 SLIDE 18 — OTHER OPTIONS
### `e18` · `ez-options` · difference · **NEW SLIDE**
### *An Honest Comparison*

👉 Address every alternative they've already been considering, before price
👉 Honest framing beats attack framing — let the facts do the work
⏱ 1:30 – 1:45

**Tone/Mood:** Even-handed and genuinely helpful. The rep is being straight about an industry that
isn't always straight. Slight warmth, no sneering. The moment this sounds like trash talk, the
homeowner starts defending the other guy.

### Block 1 — Set it up

> "Before we get to numbers, let's talk about what else is out there. Most people have already
> looked at some of this, and I'd rather walk it with you than have you wonder."

### Block 2 — Generic roller screens

> "**Generic roll-down screens.** Cheaper, and they look similar in a photo. No zipper track,
> though — so the fabric is only held at the top and bottom. In our wind it billows, gaps open
> along the sides, and the fabric works itself apart. Cheaper upfront, and you'll be looking at it
> again in a few years."

### Block 3 — Glass sunrooms

> "**A glass sunroom.** Beautiful product, genuinely. But it's a permanent structure, it starts
> around fifty thousand and goes up, and by design it traps heat. You're sitting in a glass box in
> Colorado sun — a lot of people end up air conditioning them just to use them. And you can't open
> it up when you want fresh air.
>
> Totally different product for a totally different goal."

### Block 4 — DIY

> "**Sails, umbrellas, the DIY stuff.** Fine for a season. Limited coverage, no real wind
> protection, and it comes down every fall. It's not a solution, it's a stopgap."

### Block 5 — Hurricane-rated screens

> "**And then there's the far end** — hurricane-rated track systems, things like MagnaTrack, rated
> north of a hundred and fifty miles an hour. Real products, seriously engineered.
>
> But they're built for coastal storm zones, and you pay for that. In Monument you're solving for
> afternoon wind and sun, not a hurricane. That's genuine overkill for what you're dealing with."

### Block 6 — Doing nothing

> "**And of course you could do nothing.** Which is a real option. It just means next summer looks
> like this one."

**Transition out:** "So that's the landscape. Let me show you where the money actually falls."

**Display beats:**
- Generic rollers: no zipper, held top and bottom only, fails in our wind
- Glass sunroom: $50,000+, permanent, traps heat, can't open it
- DIY sails/umbrellas: one season, no wind protection, stopgap
- MagnaTrack-class: 150+ MPH, real product, built for hurricanes — overkill here
- Do nothing: next summer looks like this one
- Even tone throughout. No sneering.

**Slow down on:** *"In Monument you're solving for afternoon wind and sun, not a hurricane."*
Why: it's the line that makes ATH the sensible middle instead of the expensive option. You just
told them not to overbuy, which is the most credible thing a salesperson can say.

**Coaching note:** Naming a real competitor product honestly — MagnaTrack is good, it's just built
for a different problem — buys you enormous credibility. A rep who claims every alternative is
garbage sounds like a salesman. A rep who says one of them is excellent and wrong for this house
sounds like an advisor.

Only bring up glass sunrooms if they're actually in play or the homeowner raises them. Don't hand
someone a new idea to go price out.

- "Patio screen means five different things" — set the frame

- Low-end installed: $1,000–$3,000/opening, manual, no zipper, 1-yr warranty

- Name their list, smile, go straight to numbers

**Slow down on:** *"We're not the cheapest quote you'll get, and honestly I'd be a little worried
if we were."*
Why: it's the most valuable sentence on the slide. Honest, slightly funny, and it inoculates
against every competing bid they'll see for the next three weeks.

**Coaching note:** This slide exists so your number isn't the first piece of price information in
the room. A homeowner with no reference point hears a price and reacts emotionally. A homeowner
who just agreed that a quality screen runs four to twelve hears a price and evaluates it. Same
number, completely different conversation.

Say the ranges with total ease. Any hesitation or apology in your voice tells them the price is
high before they've heard it. Sound like a guy explaining how the market works, not a guy
softening a blow.

The humor lives at the bottom of the range, not the top. Cheap screens are funny. Expensive ones
need to sound serious, because their job is making our number feel reasonable.

**Do not stop for questions at the end.** The energy built here is what carries into the reveal. A
pause lets it leak out and invites a pre-emptive objection before there's a number to object to.

**Callback triggers:**
- Price objection — come back and re-walk the four tiers
- "We're getting another quote"
- "That's more than we expected"

**Tone/Mood:** Confident and genuinely excited — not performed. The rep should sound like someone

> "Are you all excited?"

---

### ▸ PHASE 2 — PRESENT TWO OPTIONS ⏱ 60 sec

👉 **Primary fork is scope. Secondary fork is duty level.**

> "This option is designed to give you everything you said you wanted."

**Option 1 — The full scope.** Every opening, the recommended duty level.

**Option 2 — The priority scope.** The opening or openings that solve the biggest problem, with
the rest priced so they can add later at today's number.

> "Option one covers all [X] openings — the whole space, done at once.
>
> Option two is if you'd rather start with the [main opening], which is where the real problem is,
> and add the others down the road. I've priced those out so you know exactly what they'd be
> whenever you're ready."

👉 If duty level is genuinely in play, present it as a line within each option rather than as a
third option. **Two options, never three.**

---

### ▸ PHASE 3 — REBUILD EXCITEMENT ⏱ 40 sec

> "And the good news is you caught us at a good time."

👉 **First the one-year price. This is the safety net — it lowers pressure in the room.**

> "First thing — this price is good for a full year. You can call me tomorrow, six months from
> now, three hundred sixty-four days from now, and this is locked. I'm not coming back to tell you
> it went up. That's yours either way."

👉 **Pause. Let it land. They need to feel unpressured before you give them a reason to move.**

👉 **Then the savings. Once, with authority. Do not repeat it, do not apologize for it.**

> "The one thing that doesn't hold for a year is this — we're running `{{SAME_WEEK_SAVINGS}}` for
> folks who get on the schedule this week. That's not a pressure thing, it's just how we plan the
> install calendar. The price is protected either way. That piece isn't."

👉 **Then stop talking about it.** Moving on immediately is what keeps it from feeling like a pitch.

---

### ▸ PHASE 4 — MICRO-CLOSE ⏱ 15 sec

> "Does this all make sense so far?"

👉 Wait for the answer. This is a checkpoint, not a rhetorical question.

---

### ▸ PHASE 5 — CLOSE TRANSITION ⏱ 15 sec

> "So between the two we've talked through — which one feels like the right starting point for
> you?"

👉 **NOT** "What do you think?" or "Any questions?"

The close is a preference question. Give them a direction to move in.

**What you'll typically hear:**
- *"Let's just do all of them — we'd rather not do this twice."* (most common)
- *"Let's start with the back and see how we like it."* (a yes, not a no — write it)
- *"What's the difference if we went Super Duty on the big one?"* (a buying question — answer it and close)

---

### ▸ PHASE 6 — RIGHT-SIZING ORDER OF OPERATIONS

If the number needs to come down, in this order:

1. **Remove openings.** Let them choose which. Hold the price on the rest for a year.
2. **Step down duty level** where exposure genuinely allows it.
3. **Downgrade the warranty package** — last resort, see `e17` rep-only block.

👉 Never discount without adjusting scope first. A number that drops for no reason tells the
homeowner the first number was made up.

---

### ▸ PHASE 7 — THE STEP-OUTSIDE

👉 Situational. Primarily for appointments where both decision-makers are present and need to confer.
⏱ 5 minutes outside. No longer.

**Tone/Mood:** Completely relaxed. This only works if the rep genuinely doesn't seem to need the
answer.

> "You know what — let me get out of your hair for a few minutes.
>
> I want to double-check a couple of the track measurements and grab some detail photos for the
> file anyway. Gives you two a chance to talk without me sitting here.
>
> I'll leave everything right here — both options, the fabric book, all of it. Take your time."

👉 Leave the iPad open to the two options. Leave the samples on the table.
👉 Go outside and actually do the work.

**Coming back in:**

> "Alright, got what I needed. So — where'd you two land?"

👉 Then stop. Do not re-pitch. Do not fill the silence.

**Coaching note:** The step-outside gives a couple permission to be honest with each other, which
they will not do with a salesperson at the table. It also removes you as the thing being decided
about — the conversation becomes about the screens, not about whether they like you.

Five minutes. Not two, not fifteen. Two isn't enough for a real conversation and fifteen lets them
talk themselves out of it.

Don't use this on single-decision-maker appointments. There's nobody to confer with and it just
breaks momentum.

## `ref_close` — Pricing & Close

**Coaching note:** Urgency mechanic is `{{SAME_WEEK_SAVINGS}}` — introduced calmly, once, AFTER
the price lands and is absorbed. Never lead with it, never repeat it as pressure. Rep-only; it
never appears on a customer slide. Percentage is set at the company level, not by the rep.

**The four sales you must make:**

1. **Do the project** — they believe this space is worth solving for
2. **Do it right** — they believe the quality and warranty justify the investment
3. **Do it with ATH** — they trust you, the company, and the install
4. **Do it now** — the same-week savings and the six-to-eight-week lead time both support this

If you lose the sale, trace it back to which one wasn't closed. That's your gap.

**Accomplish List — master E-Zip version:**

☐ Use the space during peak sun hours, not just mornings and evenings
☐ Block the harsh sun from wherever it hits hardest
☐ Cut the wind so the space is comfortable to sit in
☐ Keep the view — see out without being blasted
☐ Reduce heat and glare reaching the rooms behind the space
☐ Add privacy from neighbors or the street
☐ Keep no-see-ums and mosquitoes out so evenings work
☐ Extend usability into more months of the year
☐ Push-button convenience
☐ Make it look finished and intentional, not pieced together
☐ Create a true outdoor room
☐ Something that holds up in Colorado wind long-term

## `ref_dodont` — Do & Don't

**❌ Do not:**
- Present product before the Accomplish List is confirmed and owned
- Say "95% openness" — SunTex 95 *blocks* 95% of UV and is ~5% open
- Quote 130 MPH as a Super Duty system rating — it's a fabric test, attribute it
- Present Super Duty as the default — it's an upgrade conversation
- Present motorized as an option — it's standard
- Volunteer the word "subcontractor" — reactive answer only
- Mention the PPP downgrade before the price has landed
- Bring up glass sunrooms unless the homeowner does
- Offer `{{SAME_WEEK_SAVINGS}}` before the price is absorbed
- Discount without adjusting scope first
- Ask "What do you think?" or "Any questions?" as your close
- Mention the referral program during the presentation
